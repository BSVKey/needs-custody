Water · food · shelter · communication

Proof that the water, food and money actually arrived.

In most shortages the supply exists. It fails between the donor and the person: a truck that bills for 10,000 liters and delivers 7,200, water that arrives unchlorinated, a sack of grain sold before the distribution point. Needs Custody puts a signed receipt on every handoff, so anyone can check where it failed and who signed.

Five needs, one receipt format

Water, delivered

Only safe, received liters count

A quality sensor, the truck operator and the site's water committee each sign. Liters count only if the water met the Sphere standards (chlorine, turbidity, E. coli) and the site confirmed receiving them, measured against 15 liters per person per day.

Water, bought

Pay by the milliliter

A prepaid kiosk channel bills a partial container exactly. Only the buyer's key can raise what is owed, so the kiosk can't overcharge. The newest signed state settles, so the buyer can't underpay.

Food

Loss names its holder

The receiver signs the weight at every handoff, so missing food is pinned to whoever held it. Households appear only as salted tags under one Merkle root. Each handout proves its entitlement, and double or unlisted claims are refused.

Shelter money

Drought payouts with no adjuster

A policy pays when enough independent rainfall sources agree the trigger was hit. Anyone can re-run the decision. A source that signs two values is excluded, and a payout to another address fails.

Communication

Works offline, syncs later

Field devices keep a hash-linked log with no connection. When a link returns, shuffled and resent records reassemble, and a deleted, edited or forked entry is caught.

All of it

One anchor a day

Every record of the day goes into one Merkle root in a 50-byte Bitcoin BSV output, so a single small transaction timestamps the whole day. Any record can be proven part of it with a few hashes.

Verify an anchored day yourself

A full demo day at a fictional camp of 2,400 people: six water deliveries (one short, one unsafe), a kiosk channel, food aid to 600 households with a 150 kg loss in transit, a drought trigger, and the offline log. 618 signed records, anchored on Bitcoin BSV mainnet. Your browser checks every signature, rebuilds the root, and reads the transaction from WhatsOnChain. Nothing is sent anywhere.

    What this does not prove

    • A signature is not the truth. It proves who said something. A driver and a committee who collude can still sign for water that never came, but each lie now traces to a named signer, and "safe" still needs a sensor reading.
    • Sensors drift. The quality check is only as good as sensor calibration and maintenance.
    • Salts must be guarded. Household tags are salted hashes. A leaked salt plus a guessable ID format would expose the list.
    • Demo data. The anchored day is a fictional camp with demo keys (Ed25519). Production uses Bitcoin Signed Message keys over the same record format. No field pilot has run yet.
