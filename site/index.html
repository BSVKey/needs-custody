How it works

1

Everyone signs for what they hand over

The driver signs for what was delivered and the camp signs for what it received. A water tester signs its reading. Nobody can sign for someone else.

2

Receipts work offline

Tablets in the field keep receipts with no signal and send them when a connection comes back. A missing or edited receipt gets flagged.

3

Each day gets sealed

Once a day, all the receipts are sealed together in a public record, Bitcoin BSV, for a fraction of a cent. After that, nobody can change them, including us.

Built for the basics

Water trucks

Only safe water that arrived counts

Deliveries count only if the water tested safe and the camp confirmed receiving it. You can see at a glance whether everyone got their 15 liters a day.

Water kiosks

Pay for exactly what you pour

Customers prepay, and the kiosk charges for each liter poured, half a jerrycan included. The kiosk can't overcharge and the customer can't underpay.

Food aid

Loss is traced to whoever had it

Food is weighed at every handoff, so a missing 150 kg points to one carrier. Families are checked against the list without anyone's name being published.

Drought cover

Payouts without claim forms

When enough independent rain gauges agree it was too dry, money for shelter is released automatically. Anyone can check the decision.

No signal

Made for the field

Everything works offline and catches up later, even over slow or patchy links.

Open

Free to inspect

The software is open source, so aid agencies, auditors and donors can run the same checks themselves.

What it can't do

  • A receipt isn't the truth. It shows who said something. A driver and a camp official working together could still sign for water that never came, but the lie would carry both their names.
  • Testers need upkeep. A "safe water" result is only as good as the water tester and how well it's maintained.
  • This day is a demo. Camp North is fictional, and no field pilot has run yet.
